Turn 1 · tracker pattern view · three directions

Multi-track pattern grid

Same pattern — 8 channels, 22 visible rows of 64 — three answers to the hard part: a grid that must scroll in two axes on a panel too narrow to hold it. Vertical scroll is cheap (integer row offset, whole-lane blit). Horizontal is the expensive one, so each direction spends it differently. 1a pays the horizontal scroll honestly, 1b refuses to scroll horizontally at all, 1c drops text density so all channels fit. Combinable: the cursor-row and gutter behaviour from any one drops into the others.

  • Cursor row is frozen at a fixed y — same trick as the waveform playhead. Vertical scroll is one integer offset and a whole-lane blit; no cell re-layout.
  • Twin hex gutters mean the row number is never more than four channels away, which is the reason the original had them.
  • Horizontal scroll is channel-quantised: the strip snaps channel to channel, so a channel is never half-cut and the clip rects stay aligned to cell boundaries.
  • Cost: on a narrow panel only 4–5 channels are visible at once, and the field is all text — 704 glyph blits per frame at this size.

  • Horizontal scrolling disappears as a concept — the whole song width is always on screen, and a knob slides which pair is expanded.
  • Ribbons are one 14×4 rect per triggered row: cheaper than four glyphs, and the pattern's rhythm stays readable at a glance.
  • Editing always happens in a wide, generously-spaced cell — better on a small panel than eight cramped columns.
  • Cost: you cannot compare note values across distant channels, and it is not what a tracker user's muscle memory expects.

  • Only the note reads as text in the field; volume becomes a 1–4 tick bar and any effect a single pip, so a cell is 3 glyphs instead of 11.
  • The full cell data for the cursor row lives in a permanent strip under the grid — one place to look while editing, and it never scrolls.
  • Twelve channels wide with no horizontal scroll at all; the field re-reads as pitch contour and density rather than a wall of hex.
  • Cost: instrument numbers are invisible until the cursor lands on the row, which hurts when a channel plays two instruments.

Open questions for you

  • Do you keep the original's blue-on-black palette, or bring it into the kit's own dark grey + amber palette used by the other sheets?
