Same file, same peak data, three different answers to the same question: what carries the view when zoom runs from whole-track to one sample per pixel. They are combinable — 1a is the frame, 1b is the zoom behaviour, 1c is the tracking and selection behaviour. Tell me which parts to keep.

1a

Peak field + overview rail

The reference, made honest: min/max columns, a fixed overview underneath.

drag rail = scroll · handles = zoom 1 px = 940 smp · 19 ms
  • Detail lane and overview rail are the same peak table at two decimations — the rail never re-reads the file.
  • Range highlight is a translucent band with hard edges, so a bar selection stays legible over dense material.
  • Scroll and zoom are both expressed on the rail: drag the window to move, drag its handles to zoom.

  • Three regimes, one gesture: peak columns while a pixel holds many samples, a filled envelope with a stroked edge in the middle band, and a connected polyline with sample dots once a pixel holds fewer than one.
  • The handover points are the interesting design decision — around 64 smp/px and 1 smp/px — and they can be crossfaded rather than switched.
  • Each panel keeps the lens of the one below it, so the zoom never loses its place.

Fixed head — the tape moves, the playhead doesn't

  • Tracking on: the head is welded to the centre and the file streams under it — the only thing that moves is one translate.
  • Tracking off: the head runs free, the view stays where you left it, and a catch-up chip appears once the head leaves the screen.
  • The bar band follows the transport automatically; the in/out flags are a separate, sticky sample range you set by hand.
